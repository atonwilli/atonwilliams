# The Sales Debrief, Pro

The free guide taught the method: one conversation, one skill, one drill. This pack is everything needed to run it across a floor every day without it turning into a lecture, a pep talk, or a thing that quietly stops happening in week three.

Use it in order the first time. After that, the log and the scripts are the parts you will open every day.

## 1. The ten-minute debrief SOP

Run this after every shift, or after every block of calls, with one rep at a time. Ten minutes is the ceiling, not the target. A good debrief is often six.

**Minute 0 to 1. Pick the conversation.**
Ask: "Which conversation are you still thinking about?" Not the best one, not the worst one. The one that is still in their head is the one with the lesson in it. If they cannot pick, you pick one you observed.

**Minute 1 to 4. The three questions.**
Ask them in this order and do not skip ahead.

1. What did the customer say they wanted to change? (Their words, not a summary.)
2. What did you present, and how did it connect to that?
3. What do we still not know?

If the answer to question one is "I do not know," stop. The debrief is now about the question the rep should have asked. That is the skill. Move to minute six.

**Minute 4 to 6. Name one skill.**
Say it in one sentence, in the shape "the moment was X, the change is Y." Example: "The moment was when she said the old one keeps failing. The change is to ask what happens when it fails before you show anything."

Do not name a second skill. If you have two, write the second in the log for tomorrow.

**Minute 6 to 9. Run the moment again.**
You play the customer. Start the scene two lines before the moment that broke. The rep runs it with the change. Once is fine. Twice if the first pass was mechanical.

**Minute 9 to 10. Write the two lines.**
Line one: the skill. Line two: what you will listen for next shift. Read those two lines out loud. The rep leaves. Tomorrow's debrief starts by reading them again.

## 2. The two-line log

One shared document per rep, oldest at the bottom. Every entry is the same shape.

```
DATE            2026-09-08, evening shift
CONVERSATION    Woman with two kids, asked about the price twice
SKILL           Ask what "better" means before presenting features
LISTEN FOR      A discovery question before the first feature is mentioned
```

That is the whole log. Two lines of substance and two of context. Leaders who keep longer notes stop keeping notes.

### The weekly review sheet (leader)

Every Friday, for each rep, fill this in from the log. Ten minutes per rep.

```
REP                     ____________
DEBRIEFS THIS WEEK      __ of __ shifts
SKILLS NAMED            1. ____________  2. ____________  3. ____________
REPEATED SKILL          ____________   (the same skill named twice or more is the real coaching point)
SECOND PASS RUN         __ of __ debriefs   (if this is low, the leader is naming and not drilling)
NEXT WEEK'S ONE THING   ____________
```

Two rules for reading it. A skill that repeats three times in a week is not a rep problem, it is a training gap: build a group drill. A rep with five debriefs and five different skills got five lectures, not coaching: pick one and hold it for the week.

## 3. Fifteen debrief scripts by medium

Each script is a coach's opening question, what to listen for, and the drill if the miss shows up. The customer examples are illustrative and generic. Swap in your product.

### Retail and events

**Script 1. The walk-by.**
Ask: "When they slowed down, what was the first thing you said?"
Listen for: a question versus a statement. "Quick question while you are here" beats "Let me tell you about."
Drill: five opens in a row that end in a question mark.

**Script 2. The early price question.**
Ask: "When they asked the price, what did you say and what did they say next?"
Listen for: whether the rep answered before understanding the need. Price before need is a coin flip.
Drill: rep practices "Happy to. So I give you the right number, what are you using now and what is it costing you?"

**Script 3. The silent partner.**
Ask: "Who was deciding, and who were you talking to?"
Listen for: the rep pitched the talker and ignored the decider.
Drill: run a two-person role-play where the leader plays the quiet partner. Rep has to bring them in with one question.

**Script 4. The "I am just looking."**
Ask: "Was that a reflex or a reason?"
Listen for: whether the rep can tell the difference. A reflex needs a nod and a next line, a reason needs an answer.
Drill: ten reflex objections, nod-and-go each time in under four words.

**Script 5. The yes that did not close.**
Ask: "They agreed. What did you say next?"
Listen for: a pause where the ask should have been.
Drill: three alternate-choice asks, spoken out loud, that follow the word "yes."

### Phone

**Script 6. The first ten seconds.**
Ask: "Play me the first ten seconds. What did you say before you asked for permission?"
Listen for: name, company, one reason, one question. Anything more is a monologue.
Drill: rep records a fifteen-second open five times, picks the best, leader picks one thing to sharpen.

**Script 7. The talk-over.**
Ask: "When they started to explain the problem, did you finish your sentence or theirs?"
Listen for: rep talking through the answer. This is the most common phone miss.
Drill: leader plays a customer who rambles. Rep has to stay silent for three full seconds after every answer.

**Script 8. The voicemail.**
Ask: "What did the voicemail promise, and did it give them a reason to call back?"
Listen for: a reason and a time. "I have a question about your setup, back on this line until four" beats "just following up."
Drill: write three voicemails under twelve seconds each.

**Script 9. The price on the phone.**
Ask: "When they asked what it costs, did you give a number or a range with a question?"
Listen for: a bare number with nothing tied to it.
Drill: practice "It lands between X and Y depending on two things. Can I ask those two things?"

**Script 10. The follow-up that never got booked.**
Ask: "How did the call end? What is the next specific time either of you said out loud?"
Listen for: "I will follow up" with no day and no hour.
Drill: every call ends with a day and an hour spoken by the rep, five practice closes.

### Meetings and B2B

**Script 11. The agenda.**
Ask: "What did you say the meeting was for, in the first two minutes?"
Listen for: whether the rep asked why the buyer took the meeting.
Drill: rep opens three role-play meetings with "Before I show anything, what made you take this meeting?"

**Script 12. The demo that ran long.**
Ask: "How many features did you show, and which one did they ask about?"
Listen for: showing everything instead of the one thing tied to their problem.
Drill: rep presents in three minutes with one feature only, tied to the stated problem.

**Script 13. The decision question.**
Ask: "What did they say the decision depends on?"
Listen for: the rep guessing. If they cannot quote the buyer, the skill is the question "What would need to be true for this to be a yes?"
Drill: rep asks that question in three role-plays, then says it back to the buyer.

**Script 14. The follow-up email.**
Ask: "Read me the first line of the email you sent after."
Listen for: a restated pitch versus a restated decision. The email should mirror what the buyer said the decision depends on.
Drill: rewrite the email in five lines: what they said, what we agreed, what happens next, by when, one question.

**Script 15. The stalled deal.**
Ask: "What has the buyer done since the meeting? Not said. Done."
Listen for: activity from the buyer versus activity from the rep. No buyer action after two weeks means the meeting did not land the problem.
Drill: rep drafts one message that asks the buyer to do one small thing by a date.

## 4. The four prompts

Every prompt below is complete. Paste it, then paste your material where marked. Remove customer names and contact details first. Check every quote and product claim in the output.

### Prompt A. Notes to drill (the Pro version)

```
You are helping a sales leader prepare a ten-minute coaching debrief.

Use only the information in NOTES. Do not invent customer quotes, product capabilities, motives, or reasons a sale was lost. If the notes are incomplete, say what is missing and give me the one question to ask the rep before choosing a drill.

Produce, in this order:

1. THE CUSTOMER'S PROBLEM, in their words if a quote exists. If not, label it "summary" or "unknown."
2. THE CONNECTION: how the rep tied the presentation to that problem, with evidence from the notes. If there is no evidence, say so.
3. THE MOMENT: the single point in the conversation where it broke, in one sentence shaped "the moment was X."
4. ONE SKILL, in one sentence shaped "the change is Y." Exactly one.
5. THE SECOND PASS: a role-play that starts two lines before the moment. Give the customer's opening line, what the rep should practice, and what I should listen for. Keep it under 120 words.
6. THE TWO LINES for the log: SKILL and LISTEN FOR.
7. ONE QUESTION for the next debrief.

Medium: [retail / phone / meeting]
Keep everything direct and specific. Respect a customer's decision to decline. Never recommend pressure, misleading claims, or guessing at product benefits.

NOTES:
[paste anonymized notes or transcript]
```

### Prompt B. Weekly log to coaching plan

```
You are helping a sales leader turn a week of two-line debrief logs into one coaching plan per rep.

For each rep in LOGS:
1. List the skills named this week.
2. Identify the repeated skill, if any. A skill named two or more times is the priority. If nothing repeats, pick the one with the highest impact on the customer conversation and say why in one sentence.
3. Write next week's ONE THING for this rep in one sentence.
4. Design one group drill if the same skill appears across two or more reps. Give it a name, a fifteen-minute structure, and what the leader listens for.
5. Flag any rep whose log has fewer than three debriefs this week. That is a leader gap, not a rep gap.

Do not invent conversations that are not in the logs. Do not rank reps against each other. Output one short block per rep, then the group drill, then the flags.

LOGS:
[paste the week's two-line logs, one block per rep]
```

### Prompt C. Role-play customer by medium

```
You are playing a customer so a sales rep can practice one specific skill. Stay in character until I write STOP.

Medium: [retail or event / phone / meeting]
Product category: [describe generically, for example "a monthly service plan"]
Your situation: [one sentence, for example "your current provider keeps failing at the worst moments and nobody answers when you call"]
The skill the rep is practicing: [one sentence]
Your difficulty: [easy / realistic / hard]

Rules:
- Answer like a real person in this medium. Short answers on the phone, distracted answers at a kiosk, guarded answers in a meeting.
- Only reveal your real reason if the rep asks a genuine discovery question. If they present first, get vaguer.
- If the rep uses the skill well, warm up a little. If they skip it, raise a reflex objection.
- Never coach mid-scene. After STOP, give three lines: what the rep did well, the one moment to change, and the line you wished they had said.

Start the scene with your opening line now.
```

### Prompt D. Leader self-audit

```
I am a sales leader. Below are my last five debrief logs. Audit ME, not the reps.

Check:
1. Did I name exactly one skill each time? Flag entries with two or more.
2. Is the skill observable behavior ("asked before presenting") or a vague trait ("be more confident")? Rewrite any vague ones as behavior.
3. Did I run a second pass? If the log does not mention it, assume I did not.
4. Am I naming the same skill for every rep? That usually means I am coaching my favorite topic, not what I observed.
5. Is my LISTEN FOR line something I could actually hear in the next conversation?

Give me a score out of ten, the single habit to fix this week, and a rewritten version of my weakest entry.

LOGS:
[paste five entries]
```

## 5. The thirty-day rollout

**Days 1 to 3. Leader only.**
The leader runs debriefs with two reps a day and keeps the log. Nobody else is told it is a program. The point is for the leader to get the ten-minute shape into their hands before scaling it.

**Days 4 to 10. Every rep, every shift.**
Announce it in one sentence: "After every shift we do ten minutes on one conversation." Run the SOP. Keep the log. Do the first weekly review sheet on day ten. Expect the debriefs to run long. Cut them at ten anyway.

**Days 11 to 20. Second-line leaders take over.**
If you have team leads, they now run the debriefs and you audit their logs with Prompt D. Your job moves from coaching reps to coaching the coaches. Do one debrief a day yourself so you stay sharp.

**Days 21 to 30. Group drills.**
By now the weekly sheets show which skills repeat across the floor. Run one fifteen-minute group drill per week on the most repeated skill. This is where the floor's average moves, not just the individuals.

**Numbers to watch.** Debriefs per rep per week (target: matches shifts). Second passes per debrief (target: nearly all). Repeated skill count per rep week over week (should fall). Do not measure the program by sales for the first thirty days. The sales follow the conversation, and the conversation is what you are fixing.

## 6. The debrief scorecard

Use this to grade a debrief you observed a leader run. Five items, two points each.

```
[ ] Picked one conversation in under a minute                        __ / 2
[ ] Asked the three questions in order, did not skip to advice       __ / 2
[ ] Named exactly one skill, as observable behavior                  __ / 2
[ ] Ran the second pass, leader playing the customer                 __ / 2
[ ] Wrote and read the two lines before the rep left                 __ / 2
                                                          TOTAL      __ / 10
```

A leader scoring under seven is skipping the second pass or naming too many skills. Those are the two habits that kill the program. Coach the leader the same way you coach a rep: one skill, run it again, two lines.
