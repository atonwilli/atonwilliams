# The Pitch Framework, Pro

Open, story, show, ask, lock. The free guide gave you the shape and one line per beat. This pack gives you enough lines, examples, and drills to own the shape in any medium, and to teach it to a room.

A note before the lines. Every example is generic on purpose. "XYZ Company" and "the plan" stand in for your name and your product. Use the structure, keep your own words, and only promise what your product actually does.

## 1. The five beats, in depth

### Open

The open has one job: earn the next fifteen seconds. It is not a pitch. It is a question, a reason, or both, in under ten words.

In retail and at events, the open is physical first. Stand where they can see you, face them before you speak, and ask something that requires a real answer. In a phone call, the open is name, company, one reason, one permission question, in that order, in under eight seconds. In a meeting, the open is a question about them before a slide about you.

The most common open failure is talking about yourself. "I am here today with XYZ Company doing upgrades" is a statement the customer can walk past. "Quick question while you are here" is a hook.

### Story

The story is why you are here and why it matters to them, in two sentences, ending in a question. It is not your company history. It is the bridge from "who are you" to "tell me about your situation."

The question at the end of the story is the whole point. It turns a monologue into a conversation and gets the first piece of discovery. What are they using now. What do they wish worked better. What happens when it fails.

### Show

You only present after you have something to tie the presentation to. Show one thing, tied to the one thing they said. Then check: "Does that address what you described?"

Reps lose here by showing everything. Three features is a list. One feature tied to their words is a solution.

### Ask

The ask is a choice between two real options, not a yes or no. Mornings or afternoons. Thursday or Friday. This one or that one. A choice is easier to answer, and it is honest: both options are real.

Before the ask, use a tie-down: a small question that confirms the last thing you showed landed. "That is the part that matters to you, right?" Three small yeses make the last one small.

### Lock

A yes that is not confirmed is a maybe with better manners. After the decision, say exactly what happens next, when, and who to call. The customer leaves knowing what they agreed to. The sale survives the drive home.

## 2. Forty-five lines

Three variants per beat per medium. Pick one. Make it yours.

### Retail and events

**Open**
- "Quick question while you are here. What are you using for this right now?"
- "Are you the one who handles this at home, or is that someone else?"
- "Two seconds. Has anyone shown you the new option yet?"

**Story**
- "I am here today handling upgrades for the area. What are you paying now, and what is the one thing you wish worked better?"
- "Most people I talk to here have the same two complaints. Which one is yours?"
- "We are set up here all week for the people in this area. How long have you been with the one you have?"

**Show**
- "You said it fails right when you need it. Here is what changes about that specific problem, and here is what does not change."
- "You mentioned the price creeping up. Here is what the number is, and here is what it is in a year."
- "You said nobody picks up. Here is exactly who you call and how fast they answer."

**Ask**
- "Would mornings or afternoons be better to get you set up?"
- "Do you want the standard plan or the one with the extra you asked about?"
- "Is this the address, or is it a different one?"

**Lock**
- "Here is exactly what happens: a confirmation today, the setup Thursday, and my number if anything looks off."
- "You will get a text in the next ten minutes with everything we agreed. Nothing changes until you see it in writing."
- "Two things to expect: a call to confirm the time, and me checking in after. Good call on the timing."

### Phone

**Open**
- "This is Jordan with XYZ Company. I will be quick. Do you have forty seconds for the reason I called?"
- "Jordan at XYZ Company. Did I catch you at a bad time, or do you have a minute?"
- "Hi, Jordan with XYZ Company. One question and I will let you go. Fair?"

**Story**
- "We work with businesses your size on getting the phone answered every time. What happens right now when someone calls and nobody is free?"
- "The reason I called: most owners in your line lose calls after five. Is that true for you, or have you solved it?"
- "We just set this up for a company down the road from you. Where do calls go when your front desk is busy?"

**Show**
- "You said calls go to voicemail after five. Here is what happens to that call instead, step by step."
- "You mentioned the missed calls on Saturdays. Here is what Saturday looks like with this running."
- "You said your team is drowning in callbacks. Here is what the callback list looks like after a week."

**Ask**
- "I can have it live Thursday or Friday. Which is better?"
- "Do you want me to set it up on the main line first, or the sales line?"
- "Fifteen minutes Tuesday at ten, or Wednesday at two?"

**Lock**
- "You will get an email in ten minutes with what we agreed. Nothing changes until you have seen it in writing."
- "Here is the sequence: confirmation email now, setup Thursday, and I call you Friday to check it worked."
- "I am putting Thursday at ten in writing. If anything changes on your side, reply to that email and I will move it."

### Meetings and B2B

**Open**
- "Before I show anything, can I ask what made you take this meeting?"
- "I have twenty minutes of material and I would rather use ten of them on your questions. What is the one thing you need to leave with?"
- "What would make this meeting worth the time you gave it?"

**Story**
- "We have set this up for three companies that look like yours. Where does your team lose the most time between a lead coming in and a real conversation?"
- "The reason we are talking is usually one of two problems. Leads sit, or follow-up is inconsistent. Which is closer for you?"
- "We do one thing: connect the front of the funnel to the people who close. Where does it break for you today?"

**Show**
- "You said the handoff between marketing and sales is where it breaks. Here is the handoff as it would run on Monday, with your names and numbers."
- "You said a lead can sit for two days. Here is the same lead at minute one."
- "You mentioned nobody knows who owns a lead. Here is the view your team would open every morning."

**Ask**
- "If the pilot starts the first of the month, who on your side owns it, and what do you need from us by the fifteenth?"
- "Do you want to start with the sales team or the whole company?"
- "Would you rather see a pilot on one line of business, or the full setup with a slower start?"

**Lock**
- "I will send the one-page scope tonight. You made a good call on the pilot, and here is why the first two weeks matter most."
- "Here is what happens next: scope tonight, your signature by Friday, kickoff the Monday after. Who else needs to see it?"
- "Two commitments from me and one from you. Mine: scope tonight, kickoff call booked. Yours: the names of the people who will use it."

## 3. Three worked pitches

Each pitch runs all five beats in one medium for a generic product. Read them out loud. Then rewrite them for your product without changing the shape.

### A. Retail kiosk, a monthly service plan

Open: "Quick question while you are here. What are you using for this right now?"
Customer: "The one everyone has. It is fine."
Story: "Most people who say it is fine have one thing they put up with. What is yours?"
Customer: "Honestly, it cuts out when I need it most."
Show: "That is the exact problem this was built for. Here is what changes: [the specific mechanism, accurately]. Here is what does not change: your number, your setup. Does that address the cutting out?"
Customer: "Probably. What does it cost?"
Tie-down and ask: "It is [price], and the cutting out is the part that matters to you, right? Would mornings or afternoons be better to get you set up?"
Lock: "Here is exactly what happens: a confirmation text today, the setup Thursday morning, and my number if anything looks off."

### B. Cold call, a phone answering service for a small business

Open: "This is Jordan with XYZ Company. I will be quick. Do you have forty seconds for the reason I called?"
Customer: "Forty seconds."
Story: "We work with businesses your size on getting the phone answered every time. What happens right now when someone calls and nobody is free?"
Customer: "Voicemail. And they do not leave one."
Show: "So here is what happens to that same call instead: it is answered in two rings, the caller's need is captured, and you get a text with the summary before they hang up. Would that change what happens after five?"
Customer: "It would, if it actually works."
Ask: "Fair. I can have it live on your main line Thursday or Friday, and you listen to the first ten calls yourself. Which day?"
Lock: "Thursday. You will get an email in ten minutes with exactly what we agreed. Nothing changes until you have seen it in writing."

### C. Meeting, a lead-handoff system for a sales team

Open: "Before I show anything, what made you take this meeting?"
Buyer: "Leads sit. Sometimes for days."
Story: "That is the most common reason we are in a room. Where do they sit? Between marketing and sales, or inside sales?"
Buyer: "Between. Nobody knows who owns what."
Show: "Then this is the only screen I will show you. This is a lead at minute one, with an owner, a due time, and the first message already out. Is that the handoff you are picturing?"
Buyer: "Yes. What does it take to run?"
Ask: "Two weeks and two names. If the pilot starts the first of the month, who on your side owns it, and what do you need from us by the fifteenth?"
Lock: "I will send the one-page scope tonight. Good call on the pilot, and here is why the first two weeks matter most: that is when the owners learn the rhythm."

## 4. Ready signals and the three asks

**Eight signals to stop talking and ask.**
1. They ask about price a second time.
2. They ask about timing: "how soon," "how long."
3. They ask "what happens if" about the future, not the past.
4. They repeat your line back to you.
5. They bring in the other decision maker.
6. Their objections get smaller.
7. They ask what they would need to do.
8. They go quiet and lean in.

**The three asks.** The alternate choice ("mornings or afternoons"). The assumptive next step ("so the next thing is the confirmation, what is the best number for it"). The direct ask ("do you want to do this"), used only when you have three yeses already.

## 5. The seven-day practice plan

Fifteen minutes a day, in pairs. One person is the customer.

- Day 1: Opens only. Twenty in a row, all ending in a question.
- Day 2: Story to discovery. The customer answers vaguely. Get one real detail.
- Day 3: Show one thing. The customer gives a problem; the rep shows one feature tied to it and checks.
- Day 4: Tie-downs. Three small questions before every ask.
- Day 5: Asks. Every scene ends in an alternate choice.
- Day 6: Lock. Say what happens next, when, and who to call, in under twenty seconds.
- Day 7: Full run, one medium, three times. Score with the card below.

## 7. The four prompts

### Prompt A. Pitch builder

```
Build a five-beat pitch (Open, Story, Show, Ask, Lock) for the product below in the medium below.

Rules:
- The open is under ten words and ends in a question.
- The story is two sentences and ends in a discovery question.
- The show presents exactly ONE thing, tied to a stated customer problem, and ends with "Does that address what you described?"
- The ask is a choice between two real options.
- The lock states the next step, the time, and who to call.
- Use only the product facts I give you. If a claim is not in the facts, do not make it.
- Plain language. No industry jargon. No hype.

Then give me two alternate lines for each beat.

Medium: [retail or event / phone / meeting]
Product facts: [paste verified facts only]
The customer problems this product solves: [list]
```

### Prompt B. Pitch critic

```
Grade this pitch against the five beats. For each beat: present or missing, one sentence on why, and a rewritten line if it is weak. Then give the total out of ten using this card: open ends in a question (1), story ends in discovery (1), one real detail before presenting (2), one thing shown tied to it (2), the check question (1), a tie-down (1), an alternate-choice ask (1), a lock with next step, time, and who to call (1).

Do not praise. Do not soften. Quote the exact words that lose the customer.

PITCH:
[paste transcript or script]
```

### Prompt C. Interruption trainer

```
You are a customer in a [medium] conversation about [product category]. Your job is to interrupt the rep at realistic moments so they learn to hold the five-beat structure in their own words.

Interrupt at least four times: once during the open with a reflex ("just looking" or "not interested"), once during the story with an unrelated question, once during the show with a price question, and once at the ask with "let me think about it."

Stay in character until STOP. After STOP, list the beats the rep held and the beat where they lost the structure, with the line they said when they lost it.

Begin by responding to the rep's opening line.
```

### Prompt D. Medium translator

```
Translate this pitch from [source medium] to [target medium]. Keep the five beats and the meaning. Change the length, the rhythm, and the physical cues to fit the target:

- Retail and events: short lines, present tense, one physical instruction per beat (where to stand, when to hand something over).
- Phone: name, company, reason, permission in the open; shorter shows; a spoken confirmation in the lock.
- Meetings: a question before any slide; the show is one screen; the ask names an owner and a date.

PITCH:
[paste]
```
