# Sales Terms, Pro

New reps hear these words in week one and nod. Leaders use them without checking that everyone means the same thing. Forty terms, each in four parts: what it is, the mistake new reps make with it, the drill, and the one line you can say in a morning meeting. Then the week that teaches them, the cards that keep them alive, and the quiz that proves it.

## 1. Money terms

**1. CAC, customer acquisition cost.** Everything spent to win one customer, divided by customers won. Mistake: reps think the sale is free because they did not write the check. Drill: have every rep estimate the CAC for one sale, then show them the real number. Line: "Every conversation you skip makes the ones you have more expensive."

**2. LTV, lifetime value.** What a customer is worth over the whole relationship. Mistake: chasing the first order and ignoring who stays. Drill: pick two customers, one who churned in a month and one who stayed two years, and do the math out loud. Line: "We are not paid on the sale. We are paid on the customer."

**3. LTV to CAC.** The ratio that decides whether growing is smart. Mistake: nobody on the floor knows it exists. Drill: put the ratio on the wall for a week. Line: "Three to one means every dollar we spend comes back three times. Under one means we are paying to shrink."

**4. Gross versus net.** Gross is the sale before costs and clawbacks; net is what is left. Mistake: celebrating gross. Drill: show one rep's month both ways. Line: "Gross is what you sold. Net is what you kept."

**5. Clawback.** Commission taken back when a customer cancels inside a window. Mistake: reps learn the rule the first time it bites. Drill: explain the window and the math on day one, in writing. Line: "A bad sale costs you twice: once when it cancels and once when they stop trusting you."

**6. Churn.** Customers who leave in a period, as a rate. Mistake: treating it as someone else's number. Drill: trace three churned customers back to the conversation that sold them. Line: "Churn is a sales problem wearing a service costume."

**7. Margin.** What is left of the price after the cost of delivering. Mistake: discounting without knowing where the margin ends. Drill: show what a ten percent discount does to margin on your product. Line: "A discount comes out of the margin, and the margin is where your raise lives."

**8. Payback period.** How long until the customer's payments cover what it cost to win them. Mistake: none on the floor, but leaders should know it. Line: "The first four months of a customer are the company paying to have them."

**9. Retention.** The mirror of churn: who stays. Mistake: assuming it is automatic. Drill: call two customers from last quarter and ask what almost made them leave. Line: "Retention is the follow-up we did or did not do."

**10. Pipeline.** Every conversation in progress, by stage. Mistake: counting hopes as pipeline. Drill: every rep lists their pipeline with the last real action and its date; anything over two weeks stale comes out. Line: "If the buyer has not done anything, it is not in the pipeline."

## 2. Conversation terms

**11. CTA, call to action.** The one thing you ask the person to do next. Mistake: three CTAs, so none. Drill: end ten practice conversations with exactly one ask. Line: "If you cannot say what the CTA was, there was not one."

**12. Discovery.** The questions that find the real need before anything is presented. Mistake: two questions, then a pitch. Drill: three questions minimum before any feature is mentioned. Line: "You cannot solve what you have not heard."

**13. Tie-down.** A small question after a point that gets a small yes. "That is the part that matters, right?" Mistake: forgetting them, then asking for a big yes cold. Drill: three tie-downs per presentation. Line: "Small yeses make the last one small."

**14. Alternate close.** Asking with a choice between two real options. Mistake: yes-or-no questions at the end. Drill: every close is "this or that." Line: "Mornings or afternoons is a question. Do you want it is a dare."

**15. Assumptive close.** Moving to the next step as if the decision is made. Mistake: using it before any yes exists; it reads as pushy. Drill: only after three tie-downs. Line: "Assume after the third yes, never before."

**16. Trial close.** A question that tests readiness without asking for the decision. "How does that sound so far?" Mistake: hearing "sounds good" and stopping. Drill: trial close, then one more discovery question, then the ask. Line: "A trial close is a thermometer, not a signature."

**17. Reflex objection.** A no with no noun in it. "Just looking." Mistake: answering it with facts. Drill: ten reflexes, three-word nod and a question. Line: "A reflex needs a nod, not a rebuttal."

**18. Reason objection.** A no with a specific concern. "We are under contract." Mistake: nodding past it. Drill: validate, normalize, pivot, rebuild. Line: "A reason is the customer handing you the map."

**19. Rebuttal.** The answer to an objection. Mistake: thinking a rebuttal is an argument. Drill: every rebuttal ends in a question. Line: "You are not winning a debate. You are continuing a conversation."

**20. The last few feet.** The distance between the end of the pitch and the decision. Mistake: waiting to be told yes. Drill: script the ask so there is no pause. Line: "The pitch earns the ask. The last few feet is where you actually ask."

**21. Lock (or button-up).** Confirming what was agreed, the next step, the time, who to call. Mistake: leaving on the yes. Drill: twenty-second lock, three times. Line: "A yes that is not locked is a maybe with better manners."

**22. Value rebuild.** Restating what the customer gets against the concern they named. Mistake: repeating the pitch. Drill: cost, quality, or value angle, matched to the reason. Line: "Rebuild against their word, not your favorite feature."

**23. Talk ratio.** How much the rep talks versus the customer. Mistake: 80 percent rep. Drill: record a call and count. Line: "If you are talking, you are not learning."

**24. Pattern interrupt.** A line or action that breaks the customer's autopilot. Mistake: gimmicks. Drill: one honest, unexpected question. Line: "Different is not the goal. Real is."

**25. Qualifying.** Confirming they can and should buy: need, authority, budget, timing. Mistake: pitching someone who cannot decide. Drill: one question per letter before the show. Line: "Qualify before you present or you are practicing on a stranger."

## 3. Floor terms

**26. Set.** A booked next step: an appointment, a callback, a scheduled install. Mistake: counting a "maybe" as a set. Drill: a set has a date, a time, and a confirmation. Line: "No time, no set."

**27. Show rate.** Sets that actually happen. Mistake: ignoring it and blaming the close. Drill: track it for a week per rep. Line: "A set that does not show was never sold."

**28. Close rate.** Closes divided by real conversations. Mistake: dividing by something flattering. Drill: define "conversation" in writing. Line: "Your close rate is only as honest as your denominator."

**29. Activity.** The leading number: conversations, calls, pitches. Mistake: measuring only results. Drill: every rep posts activity daily. Line: "Results lag. Activity leads."

**30. Shift.** The block of time on the floor. Mistake: partial shifts counted as full. Drill: a shift is the full block or it is logged as partial. Line: "Half a shift is a different job."

**31. Territory or venue.** Where a rep works: the event, the location, the list. Mistake: blaming the venue for the numbers. Drill: swap venues for a day. Line: "The venue changes the volume, not the skill."

**32. Ramp.** The period before a rep hits full production, usually ninety days. Mistake: judging day 30 with day 90 eyes. Drill: post each new rep's ramp week on the board. Line: "Ninety days of full effort, ninety days of full belief."

**33. Debrief.** Ten minutes after a shift on one conversation, one skill. Mistake: making it a lecture. Drill: the SOP in the debrief pack. Line: "Coach the conversation, not the result."

**34. Role-play.** Practicing a specific moment with a partner as the customer. Mistake: practicing the whole pitch instead of the moment that broke. Drill: start two lines before the break. Line: "Practice the moment, not the movie."

**35. Scoreboard.** The visible daily numbers. Mistake: a spreadsheet nobody opens. Drill: one number per role, on the wall, same time every day. Line: "A number on the wall is a standard. A number in a report is a fact."

## 4. Leadership terms

**36. Leading versus lagging indicators.** Leading moves before the result; lagging reports it. Mistake: running meetings on lagging numbers. Drill: name one leading number per role. Line: "Run the day on leading. Write the report on lagging."

**37. Override.** A leader's earnings tied to their team's production. Mistake: leaders who earn overrides and do not develop people. Drill: overrides are explained alongside the development expectation. Line: "The override is for building the people, not for standing near them."

**38. Promotion path.** The written ladder: what it takes to move up. Mistake: an unwritten path that changes by mood. Drill: publish it. Line: "If the path is not written, it is a rumor."

**39. Gate.** A requirement that must be passed before the next step: pitch certified before going live. Mistake: soft gates that everyone passes. Drill: one leader can open a gate early, on purpose, with a reason said out loud. Line: "A gate is kinder than a lecture. It is the same for everyone."

**40. Standard.** What good looks like, written, and held when it costs something. Mistake: a poster. Drill: catch yourself holding it in front of the team. Line: "Nobody rises to low expectations."

## 5. The onboarding week

Fifteen minutes a day, in the first week, with the terms as the spine.

- **Day 1: money.** Terms 1 to 10. Do the CAC and LTV math on one real sale. The point: they are paid on the customer, not the sale.
- **Day 2: the conversation.** Terms 11 to 16. Discovery, tie-down, alternate close, in a five-minute role-play.
- **Day 3: objections.** Terms 17 to 22. Reflex versus reason, the five-second test, one rebuild.
- **Day 4: the floor.** Terms 23 to 35. What a set is, what counts as activity, what the scoreboard shows.
- **Day 5: the quiz and the leadership terms.** Terms 36 to 40 explained by the leader in their own words, then the forty-question quiz. Passing is 32. Under 32, retake on day 8.

## 6. Ten morning meeting cards

Three minutes each. Read the term, tell the story, say the line, ask one rep to explain it back.

1. CAC: "The sale is not free." Story: what one conversation actually costs.
2. Tie-down: "Small yeses." Story: a close that failed cold.
3. Reflex versus reason: "Is there a noun in it?"
4. The last few feet: "Who is deciding, you or the pause?"
5. Set: "No time, no set."
6. Activity: "Results lag."
7. Clawback: "A bad sale costs twice."
8. Lock: "A yes that is not locked."
9. Ramp: "Ninety and ninety."
10. Standard: "When it costs something."
