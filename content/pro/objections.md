# Objections, Pro

Half of what sounds like a no is a reflex. The other half is a reason. Reps lose because they answer both the same way. This pack gives you the diagnostic, forty objections with the answer for each, the lines to rebuild value, and the drills that make the difference automatic.

## Start here: the build prompt

Paste everything below into Claude. Fill in CONTEXT. It asks what is missing, then builds your objection bank: forty objections your customers actually raise, each sorted reflex or reason, with the answer, the rebuild line, the stop rules, the two-week drill schedule, and a sparring partner prompt loaded with your product.

Everything after this section explains the method behind what it builds, so you can judge the output and coach with it. You do not need to read it first.

```
You are building a complete objection-handling system for my team. Read CONTEXT, then THE METHOD, then follow BUILD. Ask me up to five questions first about anything blank or unclear. Use only the product facts. Never disparage a competitor. Never recommend pressure.

CONTEXT
- What we sell and the verified product facts (does, costs, included, NOT included): [ ]
- Claims we can make: [ ]   Claims we never make: [ ]
- Medium (retail or events, phone, meetings, or a mix): [ ]
- Industry and niche (for example: home services, B2B software, staffing, fitness coaching, financial services, retail subscription): [ ]
- Who the customer is: [ ]
- The ten objections we hear most, in the customer's words: [ ]
- Competitors or alternatives customers mention: [ ]
- Our commitment terms (contract, cancellation, refund), stated exactly: [ ]

THE METHOD
The five-second test: is there a specific noun in the objection? No noun ("I am good," "not today") is a REFLEX and gets a three-word nod plus a question, never a fact. ## 1. The five-second test
```

When you hear an objection, ask yourself one thing: is there a specific noun in it?

"I am good." No noun. Reflex.
"I am good, we just signed a two-year contract." Contract. Reason.

A reflex gets a nod and a next line. A reason gets the three-line answer and a rebuild. That is the whole diagnostic, and it fits inside the pause before you speak.

The second test, for the ones in between: could they have said this to anyone selling anything? "Not today" fits every product on earth. It is a reflex even if it sounds firm.

## 2. The two answers

**Nod and go, for a reflex.** Acknowledge it in three words, do not argue with it, resume with a question. "Totally fair. Real quick though, what are you using for this right now?"

**The three-line answer, for a reason.**
1. Validate: "I hear you."
2. Normalize: "Other people in your spot felt the same way."
3. Pivot: "Here is what we found for them."

Then rebuild value against the reason they named. Not against the objection you are used to hearing.

## 3. Forty objections

Each entry: the objection, reflex or reason, the answer, and where the medium changes it. R = retail and events, P = phone, M = meetings.

**1. "I am just looking."** Reflex. "Totally fair. Real quick though, what are you using for this right now?" (R: keep your body relaxed and let them keep walking if they want. P: rare. M: rare.)

**2. "Not today."** Reflex. "No problem. One question so I know whether to bother you next time: is this something you handle, or someone else?" (R: the "someone else" answer tells you who to talk to.)

**3. "I am not interested."** Reflex, unless followed by a reason. "Fair enough. Most people say that before they know what it is. Thirty seconds, then I will let you go?" (P: this is the most common phone reflex; do not restart the pitch, ask one question.)

**4. "I am good."** Reflex. "Good is fine. Is there one thing you put up with?" (Everyone has one.)

**5. "Send me something."** Reflex on the phone, sometimes a reason in a meeting. P: "Happy to. So I send the right thing, what is the one problem you would want it to answer?" M: "I will. What should the first page cover?"

**6. "I do not have time."** Reflex. "Then I will be fast. One question: what are you using now?" If they give a real answer, you have time.

**7. "Let me think about it."** Reflex, usually hiding a reason. "Of course. So you are thinking about the right thing, is it the price, the timing, or whether it will actually work?" Name the three so they can pick.

**8. "I need to talk to my partner."** Reason. "Makes sense. What will they want to know that I can answer now so you are not carrying my message?" Then: "Would it be easier if we grabbed them for two minutes?"

**9. "It is too expensive."** Reason, cost angle. "Compared to what you are paying now, or compared to what you expected?" The answer tells you which rebuild to use.

**10. "I cannot afford it right now."** Reason, cost angle. Respect it. "Understood. If the number were not the issue, is this something you would want?" If yes, discuss timing honestly. If no, it was not about money.

**11. "We already have someone."** Reason, value angle. "Most people do. What do they do well, and what is the one thing you wish they did?" Sell the gap, not the switch.

**12. "We are under contract."** Reason, cost angle. "When does it end? And what would need to be true for you to look at options when it does?" Book the follow-up for the month before.

**13. "I had a bad experience with something like this."** Reason, quality angle. "Tell me what happened." Listen fully. Then: "Here is specifically what is different about that part." Only say it if it is true.

**14. "I have heard bad things about you."** Reason, quality angle. "What did you hear?" Do not defend before you know. Answer the specific thing, honestly, including what was true.

**15. "It will not work for us."** Reason, quality angle. "What is different about your situation?" The answer is either a real fit problem (respect it) or an assumption (show the specific case).

**16. "I do not trust it."** Reason, quality angle. "What would you need to see?" Then show exactly that, or offer a trial that costs them nothing but time.

**17. "Just give me the price."** Reason on the phone, reflex in retail. "It lands between X and Y depending on two things. Can I ask those two?" Never a bare number with nothing tied to it.

**18. "Is this a scam?"** Reason. Answer plainly: who you are, who you work for, what happens if they say yes, and what they can check. Give them a way to verify. Never joke it off.

**19. "I will do it later."** Reflex. "When is later? I ask because I will actually follow up." Get a day. If they will not give one, it was a no.

**20. "I do not make the decisions."** Reason. "Who does? And what do they care most about, so I can bring the right thing?" Then ask for the introduction.

**21. "We tried this before and it did not stick."** Reason, quality angle. "What broke: the product, the setup, or the follow-through?" Most failures are the third. Show what is different about that part.

**22. "Everyone says that."** Reflex. "They do. So let me not say it. What would you need to see to believe it?"

**23. "I do not like contracts."** Reason, cost angle. Be exact about the terms. "Here is what the commitment is, here is what it is not, and here is how you get out." If your terms are bad, this objection is right.

**24. "My cousin does this."** Reason, value angle. "Great. What does the cousin not do that you have to handle yourself?" If the answer is nothing, wish them well.

**25. "I am moving soon."** Reason, timing. "When? Depending on the answer, this either moves with you or it is a conversation for after."

**26. "I am on a fixed income."** Reason, cost angle. Respect it fully. "Then the only thing worth showing you is whether this costs less than what you pay now. Can I check that in one minute?" If it does not, say so.

**27. "Call me next quarter."** Reason in a meeting, reflex on the phone. M: "I will. What changes next quarter that makes it the right time?" If nothing, it is a polite no. P: get a date and a reason.

**28. "We are happy with what we have."** Reason, value angle. "That is rare. What are they doing right?" Then: "And the one thing you would change?"

**29. "I need to see it in writing."** Reason. "You will. What are the three things it must answer?" Send exactly those three.

**30. "Your competitor is cheaper."** Reason, cost angle. "They might be. What is included in their number?" Rebuild on quality or value, never by insulting the competitor.

**31. "I do not have the authority to spend that."** Reason. "Who does? And what is the number you can approve without them?" Sometimes the pilot fits inside their limit.

**32. "We are going through changes right now."** Reason, timing. "What kind? Some changes make this the wrong month. Some make it the right one."

**33. "I just want to enjoy my day."** Reflex, retail. "Fair. Enjoy it. If you are around later I am here until six." Let them go. Some come back.

**34. "How did you get my number?"** Reason, phone. Answer directly and truthfully. Offer to remove them if they want. Then, if they stay on, one question.

**35. "We built our own."** Reason, value angle, meetings. "Impressive. What does it not do yet that your team wishes it did?" Sell the gap.

**36. "This is not a priority."** Reason, meetings. "What is? If this would move that priority, would it be worth ten minutes?"

**37. "The last rep from your company was pushy."** Reason, quality angle. "I am sorry. That is not how this should go. Tell me what happened, and I will make sure you do not get that from me."

**38. "I need a discount."** Reason, cost angle. "What would the discount need to be for this to make sense?" Then hold your price with a reason, or trade the discount for something real (a longer term, a referral, a faster start).

**39. "I want to compare options first."** Reason. "Smart. What are you comparing on? I can tell you honestly where we win and where we do not."

**40. "No."** A clean no with no reason is a reason. "Understood. Thank you for the straight answer." Leave it open with one sentence and go. The brand is worth more than the sale.

## 4. Twelve value-rebuild lines

Pick the angle the reason points at.

**Cost (dollars and time)**
- "Here is the number, and here is what it is in a year against what you pay now."
- "The price is the same as what you have. The difference is what you get for it."
- "What does the current problem cost you in an average month?"
- "If this saves you one of those a week, it has paid for itself by Wednesday."

**Quality (how well it actually performs)**
- "Here is what happens at the exact moment it used to fail."
- "Here is who you call, and here is how fast they answer."
- "You can watch it work for two weeks before you decide it works."
- "Here is what it does not do, so there are no surprises."

**Value (what is included that they did not expect)**
- "That part is included. Most people do not know that until they ask."
- "You would get this and this, which is where most of the difference lives."
- "Here is the thing you are doing yourself today that goes away."
- "Here is what your current provider charges for this, and here is what we charge, which is nothing."

## 5. When to stop

These rules are not optional. They protect the customer, the rep, and the brand.

- A reason you cannot answer honestly ends the conversation. Say "then this is not the right fit" and mean it.
- Two clean nos in a row means you are done. Not three. Two.
- Never answer a reflex with a fact. Never answer a reason with a nod.
- Anyone who says they are on a fixed income, in a hardship, or does not understand what they are buying gets slowed down, not sped up.
- If a customer asks you to leave, remove them, or stop calling, you do it on the spot and you log it.
- The rep never decides for the customer. The rep makes the decision easy and clear, then respects it.

## 6. The two-week drill schedule

Fifteen minutes a day. Pairs. One customer, one rep. Rotate.

- Day 1: Diagnostic. The customer throws twenty objections. The rep says only "reflex" or "reason." No answers.
- Day 2: Nod and go. Ten reflexes in a row, three-word acknowledgment plus one question.
- Day 3: The three-line answer. Five reasons, validate, normalize, pivot, no rebuild yet.
- Day 4: Cost rebuild. Five cost objections, one rebuild line each.
- Day 5: Quality rebuild. Five quality objections.
- Day 6: Value rebuild. Five value objections.
- Day 7: Mixed, twenty objections, full answers, timed.
- Day 8: Medium switch. Same objections on the phone.
- Day 9: Meeting versions.
- Day 10: When to stop. The customer is genuinely not a fit. The rep practices ending well.
- Days 11 to 14: Full conversations. Objections appear where they naturally would. Score with the grader prompt.

## 8. Objections by industry and niche

The forty above are universal. Every industry adds five of its own, and they are the ones a generic bank misses. Here are six industries. The build prompt writes yours.

**Home services (repair, install, maintenance).** "I will get three quotes." (Reason, cost. "Smart. What will you compare on besides price? Here is where we win and where we do not.") "My last guy disappeared." (Reason, quality. Show who answers and how fast.) "Can you just fix it, not replace it?" (Reason, cost. Answer honestly with both numbers.) "I need to talk to my spouse." (Reason. "What will they want to know?") "It is not urgent." (Reflex until it is. "What happens if it fails in January?")

**B2B software.** "We already have a tool for that." (Reason, value. Sell the gap.) "Our team will not adopt it." (Reason, quality. Show the first week for one user.) "Security will never approve it." (Reason. Have the document ready or say when you will.) "Send me a deck." (Reflex. "What should page one answer?") "Not this quarter." (Reason, timing. "What changes next quarter?")

**Staffing and recruiting.** "We can hire ourselves." (Reason, value. "How long is a seat empty today?") "Your fee is too high." (Reason, cost. Cost of the empty seat per week versus the fee.) "We tried an agency before." (Reason, quality. "What broke: the candidates, the speed, or the follow-through?") "We only need one person." (Reflex. One person is the pilot.) "Send us candidates first." (Reason. Agree to a defined trial with a date.)

**Fitness and coaching.** "I have tried everything." (Reason, quality. "What did the last thing not do?") "I do not have time." (Reflex. "What does a week look like right now?") "I will start next month." (Reflex. "What is different next month?") "I can do it myself." (Reason, value. "What has that produced so far?") "It is a lot of money for something I might not stick with." (Reason, cost. The first-week win and the stop rule.)

**Financial services and insurance.** "I already have a guy." (Reason, value. "What does he do that you like, and what have you never asked him?") "I do not want to think about this." (Reflex. Make one small decision easy.) "Is this a scam?" (Reason. Verifiable answer, license, a way to check.) "I am on a fixed income." (Reason. Slow down, not speed up.) "Let me review it with my kids." (Reason. Offer the family call.)

**Retail subscription (a monthly plan or service).** "I am just looking." (Reflex.) "The one I have is fine." (Reason, value. "One thing you put up with?") "I do not like contracts." (Reason, cost. Exact terms, exact exit.) "Your competitor is cheaper." (Reason, cost. "What is included in their number?") "I will sign up online later." (Reflex. "What would make today the easier day?")

## 7. The three prompts

### Prompt A. Objection bank for your product

```
Build an objection bank for the product below. I want forty objections a real customer would raise, sorted into REFLEX (no specific noun, could be said to anyone) and REASON (contains a specific concern), and for reasons, tagged COST, QUALITY, or VALUE.

For each objection give: the line as a customer would say it, the classification, the answer (three-word nod plus a question for reflexes; validate, normalize, pivot for reasons), and one rebuild line that uses only the product facts provided.

Rules: use only the facts below, never invent capabilities, never disparage competitors, include at least five objections that should END the conversation and say so.

Product facts: [paste]
Medium: [retail / phone / meeting]
Customer type: [describe]
```

### Prompt B. Sparring partner

```
You are a customer raising objections so a rep can practice. Medium: [ ]. Product category: [ ]. Your real reason for hesitating, which you will only reveal if the rep handles a reflex well and asks a genuine question: [ ].

Start with a reflex. If the rep argues with it, escalate. If the rep nods and asks a question, answer it and raise your real reason. If the rep validates, normalizes, and pivots, soften. If they skip a step, repeat the objection in different words.

Stay in character until STOP. Then give: which objections were reflexes and which were reasons, whether the rep diagnosed each correctly, and the single line to change.
```

### Prompt C. Answer grader

```
Grade these objection answers. For each: was the objection a reflex or a reason, did the rep treat it correctly, did the answer contain the right structure (three-word nod plus question, or validate, normalize, pivot, rebuild), did the rebuild match the angle (cost, quality, value), and did the rep ever argue, pressure, or invent a claim. Score each out of five and give the rewritten answer for anything under four.

TRANSCRIPT:
[paste]
```
