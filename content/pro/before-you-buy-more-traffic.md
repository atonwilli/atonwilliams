## Start here: the build prompt

Paste everything below into Claude with the CONTEXT block filled in. It writes the capture, delivery, and ownership setup for your business, in your tools, with a test plan. It does not connect anything itself; it hands you the exact configuration and the order to do it in.

```
You are building the part of a lead path that sits after the click: capture, delivery, ownership, and measurement. Read CONTEXT, then produce THE BUILD in the order given. Use only the tools I name. If a step needs a tool I did not name, say so and stop at that step.

CONTEXT
- What I am offering after the click (a guide, an audit, a call, a reply): [ ]
- Where the page lives and what it can do (form builder, website platform, custom): [ ]
- Where records should live (CRM name, spreadsheet, or "nothing yet"): [ ]
- How email goes out today (provider, sender address, whether it is authenticated): [ ]
- Who owns a new inquiry, by name, and their reply window in open hours: [ ]
- What happens outside open hours: [ ]
- Consent: what the visitor is agreeing to when they submit (one line): [ ]

THE BUILD
1. CAPTURE: the exact fields (name them), the consent line, the hidden fields for source and campaign, the record the submission creates, and where it is stored. Minimum fields for my offer type, with the reason for each.
2. DELIVERY: the confirmation page text (what they got, when it arrives, what to do while they wait), the delivery email (from name, subject that repeats the promise, body, the link), and the timing.
3. OWNERSHIP: the owner notification (to whom, with which fields), the reply window, the escalation if the window is missed, and the outside-hours rule.
4. THE SYNTHETIC TEST: a fake inquiry I can submit myself, with the seven checks to run in order: record created with source, confirmation shown, delivery arrived within the stated time, link works, owner notified with the right fields, reply sent within the window, duplicate submission handled.
5. MEASUREMENT: the six weekly counts (visits to the page, form starts, form completions, deliveries confirmed, replies within window, next steps booked), how to get each from my tools, and the one decision each count is allowed to change.
6. THE ORDER: the steps to do this in, each with who does it and how long it should take. Flag anything that needs a tool, an account, or a decision I have not given you.
```

## Chapter one: the synthetic test lead

This chapter is the whole kit in miniature. One fake inquiry, walked through every hop, with the evidence you should see at each one. Run it on your own path before you touch anything else, because it tells you which of the later chapters you actually need.

### The lead

Name: Test Visitor. Email: an address you control that is not your main one, on a different provider than your sender if you can. Source: the campaign you plan to run, typed into the link by hand (for example, add `?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=audit-test` to the page address). Submit it from a phone, on cellular data, not from your office wifi.

### Hop one: capture

What you should see within ten seconds: a record, somewhere that persists, with the name, the email, the source, and the time. Not a notification. A record. If your setup only sends an email to an inbox, you do not have capture yet; you have a message that can be lost.

Evidence to keep: a screenshot of the record with the source field filled in. If the source is empty, the link did not carry it, and every campaign you run will be blind.

### Hop two: confirmation

What you should see the instant you submit: a screen that names what you got, when it arrives, and what to do while you wait. Read it out loud. If it could be the confirmation for any form on any site, rewrite it until it could only be yours.

Evidence to keep: a screenshot from the phone, not the desktop.

### Hop three: delivery

Start a timer when you press submit. What you should see: the promised thing in the test inbox, from a sender name a stranger would recognize, with a subject that repeats the promise, and a link that opens to the right place on a phone. Note the time it took and which folder it landed in.

If it landed in spam or promotions, the fix is not a better subject line. It is sender authentication, a sender the recipient has seen before, and a plain first email with one link. Log the folder; it is a count you will watch weekly.

Evidence to keep: the time to arrival, the folder, and a screenshot of the email on the phone with the link visible.

### Hop four: ownership

What you should see within the reply window you set: a notification to a named person that carries the fields they need to reply without opening anything else, and then the reply itself, to the test inbox, from that person. Time both.

Submit a second test at the worst time your business has, usually a Friday at 5:45pm. What happens to that one is your real ownership rule, whatever the document says.

Evidence to keep: the two reply times. If either is blank, chapter four is where your money is going.

### Hop five: the duplicate

Submit the same test lead again from the same phone. What you should see: one record updated, not two created, and either no second delivery or a delivery that says "here it is again." Two records with one email each is how a real prospect gets two replies from two people.

### Reading the result

Seven checks, seven pieces of evidence. Most first runs fail at hop three or hop four, and most owners are surprised, because the page looked fine. The page was fine. The path was not. Whatever failed is the chapter you read next.
