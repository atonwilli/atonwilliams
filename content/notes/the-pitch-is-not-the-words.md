<p>Every rep starts by memorizing. That is fine. You cannot improvise a structure you do not know. The problem is the reps who never leave that stage. They can recite the pitch perfectly, and customers can hear that it is a recitation.</p>
<h2>Structure, not script</h2>
<p>What a rep actually needs to own is the shape of the conversation. Open, so the customer stops. Story, so they know why you are there and start talking. Show, so what you present is tied to what they just said. Ask, so the decision is small. Lock, so it survives the walk to the car. Five beats. The words inside each one belong to the rep.</p>
<h2>How ownership happens</h2>
<p>It does not happen by repetition alone. It happens when the rep gets interrupted. A customer says something the script did not plan for, the rep has to respond in their own words, and the conversation keeps going. That moment is the transfer. Practice is where you manufacture it on purpose: the leader plays a customer who does not follow the script, and the rep has to hold the structure anyway.</p>
<h2>The leader's job</h2>
<p>Stop grading whether the rep said the words. Start grading whether they held the shape. Did they ask before they showed? Did the show connect to the answer? Did they ask for a decision or wait for one? A rep who holds the shape in their own words will beat a rep who recites the script every single week.</p>
<blockquote>Teach the structure. Let them find the words. The customer is not buying your script. They are buying the fact that someone listened.</blockquote>
