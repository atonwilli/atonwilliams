<p>For years my weeks did not end. They blurred into the weekend, into Sunday night dread, into a Monday that started with whatever was loudest. The fix was not a better Monday. It was a proper Friday. Thirty minutes, alone or with one leader, where the week gets closed the way a register gets closed. Counted, reconciled, done.</p>
<h2>Four numbers, no story</h2>
<p>Start with the numbers and nothing else. Revenue against the target. Shows against bookings. New hires against the plan. Cash against the buckets. Write them down before you talk about them, because the moment you start explaining, the numbers get softer. A miss is a miss. The explanation is for the next step, not for the number.</p>
<h2>One thing that worked, one thing that did not</h2>
<p>Pick one of each, and be concrete. A shift that overperformed and why. A rep who fell off and what the first sign was. This is where the operating lessons of the company actually get captured, one per week, and it is the part most owners skip because it feels small. Fifty of these a year is a manual nobody else has.</p>
<h2>One decision for Monday</h2>
<p>Not a list. One. The thing that, if you did it first on Monday, would move the biggest number. Write it as an instruction to yourself, with a name and a time attached. Then stop. The point of the close is that Monday already knows what it is for, so you can put the week down.</p>
<h2>What it does to the rest of the week</h2>
<p>Once the Friday close is a habit, the whole week bends toward it. Leaders bring cleaner numbers because they know they will be read. Problems get named on Wednesday instead of buried until they explode. And you start the weekend with the week behind you, which sounds soft until you have gone a year without it.</p>
<blockquote>Close the week like a register. Count it, reconcile it, and walk away from it.</blockquote>
