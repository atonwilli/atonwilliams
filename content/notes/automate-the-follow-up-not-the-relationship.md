<p>Every week someone asks me where AI fits in a sales operation. The honest answer is that it fits in about a third of it, and getting the third right is the whole game.</p>
<h2>Where speed wins</h2>
<p>The moment a candidate applies, the moment a customer asks a question, the moment a booking is made: these are moments where the fastest response wins and the customer does not care who wrote it. Reply in five seconds and you are the operation that has it together. Reply in five hours and you are the one they forgot. That is where automation belongs. Confirmation, reminder, first reply, scheduling, the reschedule when someone cancels.</p>
<p>We run all of it on the infrastructure we operate, and I would run it on a spreadsheet and a timer before I let it go back to a person's memory. The point is not the tool. The point is that follow-up is a promise, and a promise kept by a system is still a promise kept.</p>
<h2>Where trust is built</h2>
<p>The second interview. The coaching debrief. The call when a customer is upset. The conversation where a rep decides whether to stay. If a machine writes those, the person on the other end can feel it, and what they feel is that they were not worth a human's time. No amount of speed fixes that.</p>
<h2>The line</h2>
<p>Here is the test I use. If the message would be the same for a hundred people, automate it. If it would be different for each person, a human writes it, and the system's job is to make sure the human does it on time.</p>
<blockquote>Automate the promise. Keep the relationship. Most operations get it exactly backwards: a human doing the reminders, badly, and a template doing the apology.</blockquote>
<p>The setup for the first half is in the free guides. The second half is the job.</p>
