<p>Every owner has heard it. Hire slow, fire fast. And every owner has a person on the team right now that they have been unsure about for two months. The slogan is not the problem. The slogan is easy. The hard part is that firing is a decision, and most of us avoid the deciding, not the firing.</p>
<h2>Uncertainty is expensive, and it is the expensive kind</h2>
<p>A struggling rep costs a shift. A rep you cannot decide on costs the leader's attention every single day, the crew's belief that standards are real, and your own sleep. The longer the question stays open, the more it costs, and none of that shows up on a report. Deciding, either way, closes the account.</p>
<h2>Decide on evidence, not on a feeling</h2>
<p>The reason the question stays open is that it is framed as a feeling. Are they going to make it? Nobody can answer that. Reframe it as evidence. What would I need to see in the next two weeks to be confident? Not a number pulled from the air, but a behavior. Shows up early. Takes the correction and runs it the same day. Asks for the hard shift. Write it down with a date. Then the decision makes itself, and you are just the one reading it out.</p>
<h2>Deciding fast is also how you keep people</h2>
<p>This cuts both ways. The rep you are unsure about often turns out to be the one who needed a clear bar and a real deadline. When you tell someone exactly what a yes looks like, half of them go and do it, and they are better for having heard it straight. The ones who do not were never going to, and now everyone, including them, knows why.</p>
<h2>The team is watching the decision, not the outcome</h2>
<p>Crews forgive a leader who let someone go. They do not forgive a leader who let the floor carry someone for a quarter while pretending not to notice. What they learn from the second one is that standards are for other people. What they learn from the first is that the bar is real and the leader can be trusted to hold it.</p>
<blockquote>Fast is not about the day you act. It is about how long you let the question stay open.</blockquote>
