<p>Watch a new rep hear an objection and you will see one of two reactions: argue with it, or fold. Both are wrong, and both come from the same mistake. They treat every objection as the same kind of thing.</p>
<h2>Reflex or reason</h2>
<p>A reflex is what people say to end a conversation they did not choose. I am just looking. Not today. I am good. It is not about you or the product. It is a habit, and the answer to a habit is not an argument. Acknowledge it in three words and keep going. Totally fair. Real quick though.</p>
<p>A reason is different. It has a specific thing in it. The contract. The price against what they pay now. The last company that let them down. A reason is the customer telling you exactly what would have to be true for them to say yes. That is not resistance. That is the map.</p>
<h2>Three lines, then rebuild</h2>
<p>For a reason, the answer has a shape. I hear you. Other people in your spot felt the same way. Here is what we found for them. Validate it, show they are not alone, pivot to the answer. Then rebuild the value against the thing they actually named, not against the objection you are used to hearing.</p>
<h2>The coaching point</h2>
<p>When I debrief a lost conversation, the first question is which kind of objection it was. If the rep cannot tell me, the coaching is about listening, not about closing. If they can, the coaching is about the answer. Two different drills. Same word, no.</p>
<blockquote>A reflex needs a nod. A reason needs an answer. The rep who can tell them apart in the moment is the one whose numbers stop bouncing.</blockquote>
