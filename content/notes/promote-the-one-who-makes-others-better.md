<p>The easiest promotion decision is the top producer. The number is right there, everyone agrees, and it feels fair. Then the new leader spends three months frustrated that nobody else can do what they do, the crew feels judged instead of coached, and you have lost your best rep and gained a leader who does not want the job.</p>
<h2>Producing and multiplying are different skills</h2>
<p>A producer makes their own number. A leader makes other people's numbers. Some people do both, and they are rare. Most great producers succeed on instinct they cannot explain, which is exactly the thing that makes them poor teachers. Most great leaders were good, not great, as reps, and had to learn the craft deliberately, which is exactly what makes them able to hand it to someone else.</p>
<h2>The evidence is already on the floor</h2>
<p>You do not need a personality test. Look at who people drift toward on a slow afternoon. Look at whose partner had a better week than their own average. Look at who gives the correction without being asked and does it without making the other person small. Look at who stays after to run a pitch with the new hire. That behavior is leadership, before the title, and it is visible if you watch for it.</p>
<h2>Make the criteria public</h2>
<p>If promotion is about a team getting better, say so out loud and early. Tell every rep that the path up runs through the people around them. Then the ones who want it start coaching on their own, which raises the floor for everyone, and the ones who only want their own number keep their own number, which is also fine. You have simply stopped confusing the two.</p>
<h2>Keep the producer producing</h2>
<p>None of this means the top rep gets nothing. Pay them, recognize them, give them the best shifts and the first look at new things. Just do not hand them a crew as a reward. A great producer left alone to produce is one of the most valuable things an operation can have. A great producer forced into leadership is often the beginning of losing them.</p>
<blockquote>Promote for the number that grows when they are in the room, not the one they bring in alone.</blockquote>
