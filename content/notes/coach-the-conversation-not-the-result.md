<p>Most sales coaching is a scoreboard with a pep talk attached. Numbers went down, so the talk gets louder. Numbers went up, so nobody says anything. Either way, the rep learns nothing about what they actually did.</p>
<p>The fix is boring and it works: coach one conversation, not one result. After the shift, pick one customer conversation the rep is still thinking about and ask three questions. What did the customer say they wanted to change? What did you present, and how did it connect to that? What do we still not know?</p>
<h2>Why results lie</h2>
<p>A rep can close a sale after a terrible conversation because the customer had already decided. A rep can lose a sale after a great conversation because the timing was wrong. If you coach the result, you reward the first rep and punish the second one, and you teach the whole floor that the conversation does not matter.</p>
<p>The conversation is the only part the rep controls. It is also the only part that compounds. A rep who gets one percent better at asking before presenting gets that improvement in every conversation for the rest of their career.</p>
<h2>One skill leaves the room</h2>
<p>The mistake leaders make when they finally do coach the conversation is coaching all of it. Three notes is zero notes. Name one skill, run the moment again with you playing the customer, and write two lines: the skill and what you will listen for tomorrow.</p>
<blockquote>Five ten-minute debriefs beat one hour-long review. By Friday the rep has practiced one thing five times with feedback, and you have five observations instead of a feeling.</blockquote>
