<p>Watch a new rep on a floor. A shopper slows down, the rep lights up, and out comes everything. The offer, the price, the benefits, the urgency, all in one breath, because the rep is scared the person will walk and wants to get it all in first. The shopper walks anyway. Not because the offer was bad. Because they were asked to decide everything at once.</p>
<h2>A sale is a staircase</h2>
<p>Every real conversation is a series of small decisions. Do I stop. Do I answer this question. Do I let this person show me something. Do I believe this is for someone like me. Do I give my information. Do I say yes. Each one is easy on its own. Each one makes the next one easier. Stack them and you have a sale. Skip to the top and you have someone backing away with their hands up.</p>
<h2>Sell only the step in front of you</h2>
<p>The whole craft is knowing which step you are on and selling only that one. When they slow down, the job is a question, not a pitch. When they answer, the job is to earn a look, not a close. Reps who understand this are calm, because they are never asking for anything big. Reps who do not are tense, because every sentence carries the entire outcome.</p>
<h2>Find the step where conversations die</h2>
<p>Once you see the pitch as a staircase, coaching gets specific. Most reps do not have a closing problem. They have one step, usually early, where they rush or skip, and everything after it wobbles. Sit with a rep, watch five conversations, and mark where each one ended. It will be the same step almost every time. Fix that step and the close rate moves without anyone touching the close.</p>
<h2>The mindset underneath it</h2>
<p>Selling the next step means trusting the process more than the moment. It means you are willing to not close this one in order to close more of them. That is a hard thing to teach someone who is anxious about their number, which is why the leader has to model it. Ask the small question. Wait. Ask the next one.</p>
<blockquote>The close is not a move. It is what is left when every small yes has already been said.</blockquote>
