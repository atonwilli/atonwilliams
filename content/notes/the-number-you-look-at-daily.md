<p>Ask an owner what their most important number is and they will say revenue, or profit, or maybe headcount. All true. All useless for running a Tuesday. By the time revenue moves, the thing that moved it happened three weeks ago.</p>
<h2>Leading numbers and lagging numbers</h2>
<p>A lagging number tells you what happened. A leading number tells you what is about to. Revenue lags. Conversations per rep per shift leads. Hires lag. Interviews booked this week leads. Retention lags. Week-two activity leads.</p>
<p>The operation is run on the leading numbers, and the report is written on the lagging ones. Confuse the two and you will spend every meeting explaining the past.</p>
<h2>One number per role</h2>
<p>Each seat on the floor gets one number they look at every day. Not five. One. The rep has conversations. The leader has the team's practice attendance. The recruiter has interviews scheduled. The owner has the number that predicts next month's revenue, which in most sales operations is simply how many people are in the field doing full shifts.</p>
<p>When everyone has one number, the daily conversation gets very short. What is your number today. What is it going to be tomorrow. What do you need.</p>
<h2>Make it visible</h2>
<p>A number in a report is a fact. A number on a wall, or on the screen everyone opens first, is a standard. Put the leading numbers where the team sees them without asking, update them the same time every day, and let the floor police itself. It will.</p>
<blockquote>If you have to open a spreadsheet to know how today is going, today is already over.</blockquote>
