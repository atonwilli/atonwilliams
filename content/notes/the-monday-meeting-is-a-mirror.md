<p>Ask a leader what they care about and they will tell you: developing people, the standard, the customer. Then sit in their Monday meeting. Forty minutes of announcements, ten minutes of numbers, and a rushed close. The meeting is telling the truth. The leader is not.</p>
<h2>Time is the tell</h2>
<p>A team learns what matters from where the minutes go, not from what gets said. If practice gets two minutes at the end, practice is optional. If the top producer gets ten minutes of praise and the new class gets none, the new class knows exactly where they stand.</p>
<h2>The agenda that runs itself</h2>
<p>The fix is a fixed agenda that puts the minutes where the values are. Mine has not changed in years. The leading numbers, briefly, because they are already posted. One story from the floor that shows the standard in action. One skill the whole team practices, live, in the room. Who is doing what this week. Done in thirty minutes, same shape every time.</p>
<p>When the agenda is fixed, people prepare for it. The story gets better because someone was watching for one all week. The practice gets sharper because everyone knows it is coming.</p>
<h2>Look in the mirror once a quarter</h2>
<p>Every few months, time your meeting by section. Write down where the minutes actually went. Then compare that to what you would say you value. The gap is the leadership work for the next quarter, and no one had to tell you.</p>
<blockquote>Your team already knows what you value. They watched the clock. The only person surprised by the meeting is the one running it.</blockquote>
