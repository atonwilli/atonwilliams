<p>The months that scared me most were not the slow ones. They were the good ones. A big week landed, the account looked healthy, and within thirty days it was gone into things I could not name. Nothing dramatic. A little more merch, a little more ad spend, a hire I would have waited on if the balance had been lower. Money without a job goes looking for one.</p>
<h2>Assign it before it lands</h2>
<p>The fix is boring, and it works. Every dollar of revenue gets a job before it arrives, as a percentage, in a fixed order. Payroll first, because people. Then tax, set aside and not touched. Then the reserve, which is the money that lets you make decisions from calm instead of fear. Then growth, which is the ramp cost of the next hires and the tests you want to run. What is left is profit, and it is last on purpose, because it is the one job that can wait a month without anyone getting hurt.</p>
<h2>The ramp is an investment, so treat it like one</h2>
<p>A new rep costs real money before they produce. Training days, a leader's time, the paid ramp. Owners who see that as an expense flinch at it and hire late. Owners who see it as an investment ask a better question: how many days until this person pays it back, and what can I change to make that number smaller? That question leads to better training, faster gates, and sharper hiring. The flinch leads to an understaffed floor.</p>
<h2>Growth money has a limit, and the limit is the point</h2>
<p>When growth has its own bucket, you know exactly how many tests you can afford this month. That stops the two mistakes that kill small operations: spending the reserve on a gamble, and spending nothing because everything feels risky. The bucket says yes to three things and no to the fourth, and it does not need you to feel brave to do it.</p>
<h2>What changes</h2>
<p>You stop checking the balance to decide how you feel. The balance is just the sum of five jobs being done. A slow week shrinks every bucket a little instead of wiping one out. A big week grows them all instead of disappearing. And the reserve, quietly, becomes the thing that lets you say no to a bad deal and wait for a good one.</p>
<blockquote>Cash with a job compounds. Cash without one evaporates, and it takes your judgment with it.</blockquote>
